Hi-Lo Card Counting

Hi-Lo is the standard balanced count. You add or subtract one for every card you see. A positive count means the shoe is rich in tens and aces — good for you, because you make more blackjacks (paid 3:2) and the dealer busts more often when forced to hit stiff hands.

Across a full balanced deck the tags net to zero, which is what makes the count trustworthy.

2 · Running count

Start at 0 when the shoe is freshly shuffled. Add the tag of every card as it's exposed — your cards, the dealer's, and crucially every other player's cards too. The side panel on the Table tab keeps the true running count hidden behind a blur; keep your own number in your head, then hit Reveal or type your guess into Check to see how close you are.

3 · True count

The running count alone doesn't tell you much in an 8-deck shoe — a +6 with seven decks left is weak, but a +6 with one deck left is strong. Convert to the true count:

  • True count = running count ÷ decks remaining
  • Estimate decks remaining by eyeballing the discard tray, then round to the nearest half-deck.
  • The true count is what drives both your bet size and any strategy deviations.

4 · Bet spread

Bet the minimum when the true count is neutral or negative, and ramp up as it climbs. A simple, low-heat ramp:

  • True count ≤ +1 → 1 unit (table minimum)
  • +2 → 2 units · +3 → 4 units · +4 → 6 units · +5 or more → 8+ units

The edge from counting is small (roughly half a percent at decent penetration), so the money is made almost entirely by betting big only when the count is genuinely in your favor.

5 · Deviations (later)

Once the count is automatic, a handful of plays change at high counts — the "Illustrious 18." The two biggest: take insurance when the true count is +3 or higher, and stand on 16 vs. a 10 when the count is positive. Learn flawless basic strategy and a clean count first; deviations are the last 0.1%.
